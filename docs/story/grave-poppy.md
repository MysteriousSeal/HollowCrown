# Grave-Poppy

The Vale's drug, and the money under every faction's floorboards.

## The flower
- A pale poppy, white with a grey heart, that grows **only on graves**. On a fresh grave, a few stems. On a barrow,
  where the bound dead lie dreaming three hundred years, it grows thick as snow. It flowers year-round, cold doesn't
  touch it, and it smells faintly of wet earth and cloves.
- Its **milk** (the latex bled from the seed-head) is dried into amber cakes (**"amber"**, smoked), thinned with wine
  (**"the white"**, drunk) or sold raw in vials (**"milk"**).
- Folk name: *dead man's comfort*. Lantern name: *Papaver sepulcri*, "the Order's mercy".

## What it does
| Dose | Effect |
|---|---|
| A little | Pain gone, warmth, a soft sleepy joy. Used by healers (Isolde) for the dying and for surgery. |
| A dose | Bliss, and **poppy-sight**: the dead are *there*, at the edge of the eye: dead parents, dead children, speaking. The dead seem kind. They aren't always. |
| Too much | The sleeper doesn't wake; or wakes speaking in the old barrow-tongue; or wakes *wrong*, with something else looking out for an hour. |
| Habit | Weight lost, skin grey and cold, lips blue-white ("poppy-mouth"), eyes always wet. The sleeper is drawn to graves, sleeps on them, digs them to plant more. The dead begin to answer. |
| Withdrawal | Days of cold sweats, bone-ache, vomiting, and worst, **the silence**: the dead gone, the grief all back at once. Many go back for that alone. Some die. |

In the game: grave-poppy as an item gives a strong heal and a short **poppy-sight** (ghosts and hidden dead visible,
oath-cold resisted), then a debuff. Taken often, it builds a hidden **habit** (`hero_poppy` 0..5): at 3 the hero's
hands shake (aim and parry worse until dosed); at 5 the dead speak to the hero in towns, and some NPCs refuse to deal
with a sleeper. Quitting takes three days of game time of withdrawal debuffs. It's never forced on the player.

## Why it matters to the story
- **It wakes the dead.** Sleepers dig graves to plant it; every opened grave weakens the Oath there. Half the Vale's
  risen dead were woken by a spade, not by the broken crown. (Larkspur's fever, Chapel Hill, the peat bodies of Gorse
  Hollow: all spade-work.)
- **Everyone profits.** No faction has clean hands:

| Who | Their part | The secret |
|---|---|---|
| **The Lantern** | Grows it on the barrows' fringe in the **White Fields** (the Barrows, 1700–2700, 800–1100), tended by novices; sells it as "the Order's mercy" to healers | 40 years old; pays for the knights' steel. Anselm uses it to make **the Listeners** (see below). |
| **The Regency** | Taxes the trade in secret, through the **poppy purse**: the Lantern's milk goes to Carrow through Gullhaven, the Regency takes a tenth | Corvin set it up in the Wet Years to buy Carrow grain for Kingsmere, while the villages starved. It still pays the Hall guards. |
| **The Greenhood** | Sells milk to Kingsmere's poor (stolen from Lantern carts) to buy arrows and bread | Wren hates it, and does it. Her people sell to children in the Ditch. |
| **Gullhaven** | Nell Corrigan ships it over the sea and the mountains | The **Milk House**, a poppy den over her warehouse. |
| **Carrow** | Buys it by the barrel; it's banned in Carrow, and very profitable there | Sabeline's real job is the poppy contracts as much as the barrows. |

## The Listeners
Anselm's secret. Novices chosen for "a holy vigil": their eyes sewn shut ("the eyes distract the soul"), kept on
grave-poppy day and night in the **Listening Hall** under the Abbey, lying on the Order's own graves, listening to the
dead and writing what they hear by touch on wax tablets. Through them Anselm learned the barrow-tongue and rewrote the
Oath. Most go mad within a year; the dead are buried in the White Fields, and the poppy grows thicker on them.
- Found in MQ08 (one, dead, in Cairnfold's crypt, sent to listen there), hinted at by Novice Bryn (SQ-LM1), seen in MQ23.
- In MQ23 the hero finds seven alive. Choices: free them (they can't live outside the poppy: withdrawal kills two;
  the rest go to Isolde or Mother Rook), mercy-kill them at their own request, or leave them (Anselm's "work").
