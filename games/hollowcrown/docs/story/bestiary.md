# Bestiary

Every enemy type in Hollowcrown: 23 types in four families, and the unique bosses built on them. Each monster of the
Vale is a consequence of something people did: the famine, the drowning, the burning, the poppy, the gallows. Killing
one is the end of a story someone started. Understanding it is often a way to end it without a fight.

For each type: what it is, **where it comes from** (the sin behind it), how it **looks** (for its voxel model), how it
**fights**, its **weakness**, where it's met and at what levels, its variants, and a line of lore (what villagers say).

**Removed** from earlier drafts as out of place: cave worms, crabs, bats, lynxes. Their quests now use the types below.

---

## The living: wild beasts (4)

### Wolves
- **What**: grey wolves of the downs, woods and moors. Hungrier every year: the dead walking in the hills drive the
  game down, and the wolves follow.
- **Look**: lean, grey-brown, darker saddle; alphas bigger, scarred, paler muzzles.
- **Fights**: in packs of 2–6; circle and flank; one lunges while the others wait; break off when half the pack is down.
- **Weakness**: fire (torches scatter them), killing the alpha (the pack flees).
- **Where**: everywhere outdoors, levels 1–12. **Variants**: the alpha; **Old Greymuzzle** (CT-SR2), **Moonbelly** (the
  white she-wolf, CT-LM1).
- *"Wolves never used to come down past the Nine Sisters. Now they sleep in the hay."*

### Boars
- **What**: wild boars of the woods and field edges; passive until struck.
- **Look**: black-bristled, heavy shoulders, yellow tusks; old boars grey on the snout.
- **Fights**: charge in a line, turn slowly; a stunned boar is open from behind.
- **Weakness**: sidestep the charge.
- **Where**: Brindle Vale, Southfields, Greenwood edges, levels 2–10. **Variants**: **Old Tusk** (CT-SF1).
- *"A boar's a farmer's curse and a lord's supper."*

### Bears
- **What**: brown bears of the deep wood; rare, solitary.
- **Look**: huge, shaggy brown, a pale chest blaze.
- **Fights**: a swipe, a slam that knocks down, a rearing roar that stuns briefly; charges if the hero runs.
- **Weakness**: slow to turn; keep moving round it.
- **Where**: the Greenwood, the north shore downs, levels 8–12. **Variants**: **Old Scar** (CT-GW1).

### Spiders
- **What**: giant spiders of old mines and caves, grown big on the dead the cold drives into their holes.
- **Look**: hairy, mottled grey-brown, eight eyes like wet beads; brood mothers swollen, pale-bellied.
- **Fights**: drop from ceilings, spit webs (slow), bite (poison); spiderlings swarm.
- **Weakness**: fire burns webs and egg-sacs; light keeps them off.
- **Where**: Mossjaw Cave, Spinner's Deep, levels 2–11. **Variants**: spiderling, **brood mother**, **the Spinner**.
- *"Where there's webs there's bones. Where there's bones there's the dead. Spiders know the dead are coming."*

## The living: people (5)
Each faction fights its own way. All can surrender at low health (most bandits do; knights almost never).

### Bandits
- **What**: robbers, wreckers, raiders, deserters, grave-robbers, rogue hoods: the Vale's desperate and its cruel.
- **Look**: mismatched gear, hoods, rags over leather; each band its sign (the Red Hen's red feathers, the Weed Rats'
  reed cloaks, the wreckers' salt-white coats, the Sour Apple's green hoods over Carrow coin).
- **Fights**: cowards in a pack: rush together, scatter when the leader falls; some throw knives; some carry milk and
  fight harder, and can't feel pain, for a minute.
- **Where**: every camp, levels 2–13. **Variants**: knife-thrower, brute, **chief** (each band's: Brannoc, Lanky Moll,
  Old Sorrel, Corliss Crabbe, Hugh Hobday, Haskel the Spade).

### Regency herons
- **What**: the Regent's soldiers: pressed farm boys and hard veterans, grey heron on the tabard.
- **Look**: kettle helms, grey tabards with the heron, mail, round shields; crossbowmen in padded coats.
- **Fights**: shield walls of three, a crossbow line behind; they call for help (more come within a minute in towns).
- **Weakness**: break the wall from the side; the crossbows reload slowly.
- **Where**: Kingsmere, Thornbeck, checkpoints, patrols, levels 4–16. **Variants**: crossbowman, sergeant, **Hall guard**
  (Captain Brayne's, better armour).

### Greenhood hoods
- **What**: Wren's outlaws: hunters, deserters, the starved.
- **Look**: green hooded cloaks, leather, longbows, bracers; faces smeared with charcoal on raids.
- **Fights**: ambush from cover; archers keep their distance and move after each shot; a few with long knives close in.
- **Weakness**: they break and run if surprised; poor in the open.
- **Where**: the Greenwood, the Wood Road, raids, levels 6–15. **Variants**: archer, knife-man, **Pip Tanner**.

### Lantern knights
- **What**: the Order's knights, sworn to fight the dead; rarely fight the living, never back down when they do.
- **Look**: full plate, a brass lantern on the breast, grey surcoats, long-hafted maces and oath-iron-banded swords.
- **Fights**: heavy, slow, relentless; their oath-iron weapons hit the dead much harder and the living hard enough; one
  kneels and prays to heal the others once a fight.
- **Weakness**: slow; stamina runs out; the healer first.
- **Where**: the Abbey, patrols, MQ20's writ, MQ23, levels 8–16. **Variants**: novice-at-arms (light), knight,
  **knight-sister** (Odalys's own), **forge-brother** (Aedric).

### Carrow mercenaries
- **What**: Duke Ferrand's paid soldiers: Sabeline's guards, couriers, prospectors' escorts, the army over the pass.
- **Look**: Carrow blue and black, steel half-plate, plumed sallets; pikes and arbalests.
- **Fights**: pike lines that punish a charge; arbalests that pierce shields; disciplined, they retreat in order.
- **Weakness**: get inside the pikes.
- **Where**: Kingsmere (Envoy's House), Coldstep Pass, the Barrows' adit, the siege (if the pass is open), levels 11–17.
  **Variants**: pikeman, arbalester, **captain**.

## The dead of the Oath (6)
The dead who stir because the crown is broken and their graves are opened. They're bound: most can be laid to rest with
the **Rite of Rest** (SQ-BV4) or the **true rite** (SQ-LM4), if the hero knows what holds them.

### Ghosts
- **What**: the restless dead who still want something: a person, a place, a word.
- **Look**: pale blue-white, translucent, the shape of who they were; edges that fray like smoke.
- **Fights**: drift through walls, a cold touch that slows, a scream that frightens (the hero's blows weaker briefly).
- **Weakness**: oath-iron, lantern light, the Rite of Rest, or giving them what they want.
- **Where**: everywhere there's a story, levels 1–15. **Variants**: shades (weak), wailing ghosts, named ghosts (Rhosyn,
  Lissa, Ambrose Fair, Sir Lowell, Corporal Ashdown).

### Skeletons
- **What**: the old dead of the crypts, bones held together by the Oath's fraying.
- **Look**: brown-yellow bones, scraps of grave-cloth, rusted arms; archers with bows of black yew.
- **Fights**: soldiers in lines, archers behind; they reassemble once unless broken by a blunt blow or oath-iron.
- **Weakness**: maces and hammers; oath-iron.
- **Where**: every crypt, levels 2–14. **Variants**: archer, guard, **dead knight** (the Undercroft, the Watchers).

### Draugr
- **What**: the barrow-dead in their iron: Hrathgar's people, buried with their arms, and the peat's bog-bodies.
- **Look**: blackened flesh on huge frames, barrow-iron armour grown through with roots, glowing cold eyes; bog-draugr
  brown and leathery, rush nooses at their necks.
- **Fights**: slow, enormous blows that break guards, a breath of frost that slows; they don't flinch.
- **Weakness**: fire; the old barrow-tongue greeting (`cairn_names`) makes some hesitate.
- **Where**: the Barrows, the peat bodies of Gorse Hollow, the Grey Stones, the Toll Bridge, levels 7–18. **Variants**:
  bog-draugr, barrow-wight, standard-bearer, the Toll-Keeper.

### Barrow-hounds
- **What**: the barrow-dead's hunting dogs, buried with their masters.
- **Look**: wolf-skeletons with scraps of hide and iron collars, cold light in the eye-sockets.
- **Fights**: fast packs that run down anyone who flees; they howl to call their master.
- **Weakness**: their master's horn; blunt blows.
- **Where**: Hob's Tower (CT-BV1), the Hunter's Barrow, the Barrows, levels 3–15. **Variants**: skeleton hound, the
  **Kennel-Master**'s pack.

### Barrow-giants
- **What**: Hrathgar's hall-guard, the tallest of his warriors, roots grown through them over three centuries until
  they're more oak than man.
- **Look**: three times a man's height, bark and bone and iron, a crown of roots.
- **Fights**: sweeping blows over a wide arc, a stamp that knocks down, they tear up stones to throw.
- **Weakness**: fire; their roots (the legs) can be cut.
- **Where**: the siege, the Barrow Valley, the Great Barrow, levels 16–18.

### Crypt lords
- **What**: the boss of each crypt: the dead who were someone, and still are.
- **Fights**: each its own (see its quest): Sir Hamund (a knight's duel), Abbess Wynfrith (calls skeletons), Lord Osbert
  Hale (drowned, drags into water), King Edric (the Drowned King), Old Mother Grisel (rot and Bloomers), Ulfar (an
  archer who hides), Sir Eadric Long (the First Watcher), the Grey Rider, and Hrathgar.

## The Vale's horrors (8)
Creatures that exist because of what the living did. Each is new to Hollowcrown.

### The Hungry (famine-wraiths)
- **From**: the Wet Years. The starved dead, buried without rites in pits and hedgerows while the reeves took the grain.
- **Look**: ghosts thin as sticks, swollen bellies, hollow cheeks, rags; women carrying children; some still holding a
  crust.
- **Fights**: they don't attack first: they go to houses, larders, barns. Stopped, they claw, and their touch drains
  stamina and hunger (the hero needs food sooner). In numbers, they mob.
- **Weakness**: food left at their graves calms them; burial with rites ends them; fire scatters them.
- **Where**: the famine pit (MQ01), the hedge graves of Millbrook (CT-SF1), Maud's grave (SQ-GW6), Hesk's lane, levels
  1–10.
- *"Leave bread on the step. Not for the dead. For you, so they pass you by."*

### The Drowned
- **From**: the lake and the sea: those who drowned and weren't found. Hollowmere's dead of the Still Water; the
  wreckers' victims; those who walked into the water.
- **Look**: bloated, white-blue, waterlogged clothes, weed in the hair, eyes clouded like a fish's; they drip.
- **Fights**: they wait under the water and grab: a hold that drags the hero under (break free or drown); slow on land,
  dangerous by the shore.
- **Weakness**: they can't leave the water's edge for long; fire dries them; in the light of a lit lighthouse they sink.
- **Where**: the Drowned Chantry, Gullmouth, the Weeping Willows, the Gannet Rocks' wreck, Wrecker's Hole, the siege's
  harbour, levels 8–16. **Variants**: drowned sailor, drowned courtier, **the Tide-Wife** (CT-SR1).
- *"Don't swim at dusk. The water's got hands at dusk."*

### The Ashen
- **From**: the Night of Lanterns. The four thousand burned at Cairnfold, Hrathgar's women, children and old folk.
- **Look**: charred black figures still smouldering, embers in the cracks of their skin, ash falling from them; small
  ones among them.
- **Fights**: they walk toward the hero burning; their touch sets the hero alight; they scream when struck, a sound that
  stops other enemies too. They **never attack a Barrowborn** (allies of Barrowborn blood are ignored).
- **Weakness**: water; the true names (`cairn_names`) spoken: they stop and listen; nothing else stops them for good.
- **Where**: Cairnfold's ruin at night (after MQ22), the Hall of the Wives in the Great Barrow, the siege (they walk
  past Kingsmere's walls toward the Ditch, not to attack it, to stand round it: a scene), levels 10–18.
- *"The grey-eyes say the Ashen are coming home. We say they're coming for us. We might both be right."*

### Bloomers
- **From**: the poppy. Corpses the poppy grew through: diggers who died on the barrows they planted, Listeners buried in
  the White Fields, the Gardener's dead. The poppy moves them, in its dream.
- **Look**: bodies split by white poppy stems, flowers blooming out of mouths and eye-sockets, roots trailing; they
  sway like flowers in wind.
- **Fights**: slow, rooted in place until the hero is near; they burst clouds of poppy-pollen that give **poppy-sight**
  and confusion (the hero sees enemies that aren't there; real ones turn invisible for a moment); lash with stems.
- **Weakness**: fire (they go up fast); cutting the stems at the root.
- **Where**: the White Fields, Larkspur Barrow (the diggers), the Abbey's poppy terraces, the famine pit's poppy,
  levels 9–16. **Variants**: Bloomer, **the Gardener's Bloom** (a mass of bodies grown together; SQ-BR6 if the fields
  burn badly).
- *"Where the white grows thick, don't sleep. You'll wake up planted."*

### The Hollowed
- **From**: the poppy, still living. Sleepers too far gone: the milk has emptied them and something from the graves
  looks out through them.
- **Look**: grey skin, blue-white lips, wet unblinking eyes, dirt to the elbows from digging; rags; they hum.
- **Fights**: harmless by day (they sleep on graves); at night, in packs, they claw and bite, feel nothing, and speak
  with the dead's voices (*"Mam? Is that you?"*). They're people: killing them is killing people; most can be knocked
  down and left, and some can be brought back (Isolde, Nan Wicket, Mother Rook).
- **Weakness**: grave-poppy itself: a vial thrown at their feet and they kneel to it; daylight.
- **Where**: the Ditch's cemetery, the Milk House's back rooms, Chapel Hill at night, Larkspur's barrow, the Old Road
  (CT-GW2), levels 4–14.
- *"They're not dead. That's the worst of it."*

### The Sewn
- **From**: the Listeners. Novices whose eyes Anselm sewed shut and fed poppy until the dead's voices drowned their own;
  most died, some didn't, and wander the Undercroft, the White Fields, the moor.
- **Look**: thin, young, grey robes, eyes stitched with black thread, wax tablets hung round their necks, fingers worn
  raw from writing blind.
- **Fights**: they hunt by sound: the hero sneaking is invisible to them, running or fighting draws them; they shriek
  barrow-tongue, which calls every dead thing near; they fight with tablets' iron styluses.
- **Weakness**: silence; a cut thread (opening the eyes) stuns them and may wake them (a choice: mercy or capture).
- **Where**: the Abbey Undercroft (MQ23), the White Fields, the Black Fen at night, levels 12–16.
- *"The novices on the moor at night. Don't answer them. They're not asking you."*

### Pit-eaters
- **From**: the Wet Years. People who survived the famine by eating the dead, and found they couldn't stop: half-wild,
  living in caves, tunnels and the old pits, eating what the dead leave and what the living drop.
- **Look**: gaunt, long-nailed, filthy, cloth-wrapped, quick and crouching; some with old family keepsakes on strings.
- **Fights**: ambush in darkness; fast hit-and-run; they drag the fallen away (an ally can be carried off and must be
  rescued); flee from light.
- **Weakness**: light (lanterns, fire); they won't cross salt (an old belief that's true).
- **Where**: the tunnels under Millbrook's granary (CT-SF2), Mossjaw's first chamber, Gullmouth's upper cave, the Ditch's sewers,
  levels 6–14.
- *"In the Wet Years we didn't ask where the Cobbes' meat came from. We should have."*

### Carrion rooks
- **From**: the gallows. Rooks and ravens fat on hanged men: Kingsmere's gallows, the Hanging Oak, the gibbets on the
  roads, the battlefields of the barrow hills. They've learned people are food.
- **Look**: black rooks bigger than they should be, bald-faced, oily feathers; in flocks of a dozen to forty.
- **Fights**: a flock dives at the eyes (blinding briefly), pecks, scatters, comes back; they follow the hero for
  miles once they've tasted blood (the hero bleeds: a wound that draws them).
- **Weakness**: fire and noise (a horn); killing the flock's leader (the oldest, white-eyed).
- **Where**: Gallows Square at dusk, the Hanging Oak, the gibbets, the Barrows, the siege's aftermath, levels 3–16.
- *"The herons hang them; the rooks bury them."*
